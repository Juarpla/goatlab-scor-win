## Club revenue and transfer flows

Deloitte's 2026 Football Money League compares the 2024/25 reporting period. The leading 20 men's clubs generated €12.4bn in total revenue (+11%). Real Madrid led at roughly €1.2bn, followed by Barcelona (€975m), Bayern Munich (€861m), Paris Saint-Germain (€837m) and Liverpool (€836m). Commercial revenue accounted for €5.3bn (43%), matchday revenue for €2.4bn (19%) and the three streams also include broadcasting. These are revenue measures, with player transfer fees excluded under Deloitte's methodology. [Deloitte release](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/real-madrid-tops-deloitte-football-money-league.html) · [Deloitte report and methodology](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/services/financial-advisory/analysis/deloitte-football-money-league.html)

In its newer 2025/26 accounts, Real Madrid reported €1,221.3m operating revenue before disposals (+3.1%) and €26.3m profit after tax. Its official breakdown gives €363m stadium revenue, €319m television and international competition revenue, and €539m marketing revenue. Barcelona reported €1.06bn ordinary revenue, €0.2m ordinary profit and an €18m net loss after extraordinary items and taxes. The latest club accounts use a later season and their own definitions compared with Deloitte's cross-club comparison. [Barcelona 2025/26 accounts summary](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/club/news/4578647/assembly-approves-record-breaking-accounts-that-confirm-clubs-financial-recovery) · [Real Madrid 2025/26 accounts summary](https://www.realmadrid.com/es-ES/noticias/club/actualidad/el-real-madrid-cierra-la-temporada-2025-2026-con-los-mayores-ingresos-y-el-mayor-ebitda-de-su-historia-28-07-2026)

FIFA reported 86,158 international transfers in calendar 2025 across professional and amateur men's and women's football. Total spending hit US$13.11bn, up by more than 50% from 2024. Men's professional spending was US$13.08bn and women's professional spending US$28.6m (+80% or more), with 2,440 women's professional moves. Men’s professional clubs in England spent US$3.82bn and received US$1.77bn, first in both categories. Brazil recorded 1,187 arrivals and 1,000 departures in the men's professional table. Argentina spent US$133m and received US$217m, and Ecuador spent US$2.52m and received US$42m. These figures cover transfers between associations. [FIFA release](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/media-releases/international-transfers-reach-historic-high-2025) · [FIFA men's transfer table](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/transfer-reports?tab=Men%27s+global+transfer+report+2025)

Men's professional clubs spent over US$9.89bn on more than 12,500 international moves, with English clubs leading spending at over US$3.02bn and French clubs leading receipts at US$1.7bn. Women's professional clubs spent US$28.6m on 1,406 international deals, with spending more than double the preceding midyear window. ## Club World Cup and solidarity

FIFA's annual report confirms the 2025 Club World Cup distributed US$1bn to 32 participants, with US$525m in participation payments and US$475m in performance payments. The champion's possible maximum was about US$125m. CONMEBOL participation earned a club US$15.21m while UEFA awards varied from US$12.81m to US$38.19m by sporting and commercial rank. Performance payments included US$2m per group win and US$1m per draw, then cumulative qualifying awards of US$7.5m, US$13.125m, US$21m, US$30m for the runner-up or US$40m for the winner. Chelsea won the tournament. Tournament solidarity for non-participants had a cap of US$250m and FIFA's year-end accounts described implementation as ongoing. [FIFA distribution model](https://inside.fifa.com/es/organisation/news/mundial-clubes-fifa-2025-premios-record-programa-solidaridad-sin-precedentes) · [FIFA 2025 annual report](https://inside.fifa.com/es/official-documents/annual-report/2025/financials/2025-financials-in-review/fifa-club-world-cup-2025) · [FIFA financial statements, note 30](https://vod.fifa.com/official-documents/annual-report/2025/financials/2025-financial-statements/notes/30-reserves)

Under FIFA's transfer solidarity mechanism, 5% of qualifying transfer compensation for a professional moved under contract is allocated across eligible clubs that developed the player from age 12 through 23, prorated by registration. In a full year each age 12–15 contributes 0.25% of transfer compensation and each age 16–23 contributes 0.5%. A hypothetical €10m eligible fee makes a €500,000 solidarity pool. The Clearing House uses an electronic player passport to identify eligible training clubs and distributes payments. Training compensation is a separate category under FIFA's regulations, as is the Club World Cup's tournament solidarity. [FIFA regulations, Annex 5](https://img.fifa.com/image/upload/fs7qzlzwuekhfcc339zx.pdf) · [FIFA Clearing House](https://inside.fifa.com/transfer-system/clearing-house/regulations-and-explanatory-notes)

By July 2026 FIFA reported almost US$1bn in combined training rewards allocated since the Clearing House launched in November 2022, with more than US$639m distributed. Career transfers involving 530 players called up for the 2026 World Cup brought about an allocation of US$221m across training compensation and solidarity. [FIFA Clearing House release](https://inside.fifa.com/subuniverse-clearing-house/media-releases/world-cup-2026-shines-spotlight-clearing-house-training-rewards-1-billion-usd-milestone)
