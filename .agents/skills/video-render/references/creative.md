# Creative direction

This is the single source of creative direction for final GoatLab videos. Read it
before planning an edit and use it again during visual review. Fixed identity is
in [brand.md](brand.md); JSX, props, timing, safe areas and tools are in
[remotion.md](remotion.md). The workflow lives in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).
Update creative preferences here; other documents should link here rather than
introduce their own aesthetic rules. Historical reports describe past systems.

## Intent

Make a contemporary football piece whose images and clips carry the experience.
The viewer should remember the football and understand the argument. Graphics
orient attention, clarify a comparison or identify a fact while the visual action
remains visible and recognizable. Photos can carry an entire passage; clips can
carry another. Their role follows the narration and the quality of the material.

Modernity comes from precise editing, confident framing, clear hierarchy and
purposeful motion. Complexity is optional. Choose a distinct visual idea for each
audio rather than a reusable sequence of title, card, clip and closing.

## Find the edit in the material

Listen to the complete recording and inspect the available photos and clips.
Identify the hook, changes in the argument, evidence and projection in the voice.
Choose visual responses to those moments; script beat labels do not prescribe
scene boundaries or layouts. Describe the concept and the media selection briefly
in `notes` alongside the plan's existing fields, so a later revision preserves
intent. These notes are for the author, not visible video copy.

Select media for relevance, visual quality and continuity. There is no quota of
clips, photos, cuts or on-screen figures. A production target of twelve images and
six clips describes the bank, not an obligation to show them all. Omit redundant,
weak or distracting assets and note consequential omissions. With a partial bank,
build around what works; report missing material. If nothing is usable, complete
the bank before rendering this media-led format.

Generated images and clips illustrate the discussion. They do not document real
plays, a result, a lineup or an identifiable player's participation. Keep factual
claims and projections distinct under [COMPLIANCE.md](../../../../COMPLIANCE.md).
Show statistics only when they clarify the spoken argument, with their meaning,
sample and source available from `facts`; the recording need not acquire extra
numbers to satisfy a visual quota.

## Keep the action in view

Compose around the subject in each shot: players, ball, duel, goalmouth or fans.
Protect those subjects through crops, transitions and overlays, including their
movement during the whole shot. An empty corner at the opening may become the
point of action later. Inspect moving clips beyond a single reference frame.

Let strong shots occupy the canvas. Use an inset, split screen or collage when
it reveals a relationship; keep each image large enough to read on a phone.
Place graphics in genuine free space and remove them when their point is made.
If text cannot fit without obscuring the action, change its placement, wording,
shot or timing. A large opaque panel should not turn football into wallpaper.

Give attention a clear order: the visual action, synchronized captions and the
occasional explanatory cue. A statistic, label, line or small comparison can
support a passage. Avoid competing headlines, counters, badges and captions
asking the viewer to read several messages at once. Use a graphic emphasis as a
brief event and return attention to the media.

## Rhythm and motion

Let the opening image carry the hook immediately. Build rhythm from spoken
emphasis, a change of argument, movement in a clip or a meaningful reveal. Hold a
shot when it deserves attention; cut when the next image adds something. Neither
constant speed nor a fixed cut interval defines the style.

Connect shots through direction of movement, framing, a shared shape or a change
of scale when that serves the idea. A direct cut can be the strongest transition.
Use transitions to carry attention into the next image and settle quickly enough
to see it. Keep a recognizable shot between effects.

Animate photos with considered reframing, camera movement, an unexpected crop or
an interaction with another image. Preserve the subject and use different motion
only when the content calls for it. The clip's own movement may already be enough;
extra zoom, shake and distortion must earn their place. Reuse media with a new
narrative purpose; avoid conspicuous loop seams and prolonged frozen action.

Time supporting graphics to the relevant words. Their entrance, emphasis and exit
should help comprehension. A measured reveal or small movement is often enough.
SVG, masks, parallax, typography and compositing remain available tools, not an
effects checklist. Choose the treatment from the material instead of demonstrating
every available technique.

## Image and readability

Keep color vivid, skin and uniforms credible, highlights controlled and shadows
open. Preserve the character of each image rather than forcing a global tint.
Use the brand accent selectively; it need not wash over the football. Dark green
belongs to the fixed closing identity and should not dominate the content.

Keep the subject sharp. Use blur for separated backgrounds or a brief transition.
Fit media to the delivery canvas with restrained crops. A 720p clip necessarily
scales for 1080p delivery; allow that fit and judge the result, while avoiding
additional zoom that makes faces, the ball or uniforms visibly soft. Normalized
photo dimensions, rather than a nominal original resolution, set the crop budget.

Keep one synchronized caption layer with the identity defined in
[brand.md](brand.md). Compose captions and supporting text within the technical
safe area, away from the important action. Use compact line breaks and enough
hold time to read. Obtain contrast with the outline or a small local treatment;
keep the rest of the image bright and visible.

## Review the result

Apply this direction to the rendered video, not only to the JSX. Inspect the
workflow's sample frames, then watch motion around cuts, overlays and repeated
clips with the voice. Frames alone cannot demonstrate rhythm or synchronization.

Check that the action stays recognizable throughout each shot, graphics clarify
rather than compete, captions read comfortably, and crops preserve image quality.
Check that each visual change has a reason in the audio or the material. Remove
layers or revise the edit where attention becomes confused. A valid render still
needs this creative review.

When recent renders are available, compare the concept, opening, framing and
rhythm. Preserve recognizable identity while varying the composition in response
to this audio. No mandatory style rotation, percentage of screen coverage or scene
template substitutes for watching the piece.
